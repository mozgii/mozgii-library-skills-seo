# Product reviews, best lists and comparison pages

Review content (a single review, a "best X" list, an "X vs Y" comparison, an "alternatives to X" page) is judged by
Google's reviews system, which runs continuously as part of ranking and rewards reviews written from real use. The
question it asks is whether the page helps a buyer decide better than the manufacturer's page and other reviews do.

## What a review page needs

A review page earns trust with evidence of first-hand use that a reader can see. Google's guidance on high-quality
reviews boils down to the list below.

- Proof of use: your own photos, video, audio or measurements of the product, not press images.
- Numbers: weight, battery hours, load times, prices on a date, test results with the method.
- What sets the product apart from others in its class, and how it compares with the ones a buyer also considers.
- Benefits and drawbacks, for whom it suits and for whom it does not.
- What changed from the previous model or version, when that matters to buyers.
- The factors that decide the purchase in this category, and how the product scores on each.
- The reviewer's experience with the category, shown in the byline and author page.
- Links to more than one seller when it helps the reader choose where to buy.

## Best lists that rank

A "best X" list is a set of short reviews plus a ranking, so each entry needs its own evidence and the order needs a
reason.

- State the method: what was tested, how, over what time.
- One section per product: who it is best for, the measured facts, the main drawback.
- Say why number one beats number two in one sentence.
- Date the list and update it when products or prices change; a list of discontinued items loses trust fast.

## Comparison and alternatives pages

A comparison page ("A vs B") and an alternatives page ("alternatives to A") answer a buyer who already has a
shortlist. They work when they are fair.

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| a feature and price table from tested or sourced facts, with dates | marketing claims copied from each vendor |
| who should pick A and who should pick B | a verdict that always picks your own product |
| real reasons people switch from A, with sources | invented weaknesses of a competitor |
| your own product placed where it honestly fits, labelled as yours | hiding that the page is written by a vendor |

Comparison pages for many pairs at scale are programmatic pages and need data unique to each pair (the
`programmatic-seo` skill).

## Affiliate links and disclosure

Affiliate review sites follow two rules. Links that earn a commission carry `rel="sponsored"` (or `nofollow`), and the
page tells readers that it earns from purchases, near the top. A page that only repeats the merchant's description
with an affiliate link is thin affiliation under Google's spam policies, however many such pages there are.

## Review brief

The review brief is filled before writing, so the evidence exists before the text.

```text
Product(s):          <name, model, version>
Buyer and decision:  <who is buying, what they must decide>
Test method:         <what was done, for how long, with what tools>
Measurements:        <numbers to collect>
Own media:           <photos and video to shoot>
Compared with:       <2–4 products the buyer also considers>
Decision factors:    <3–6 factors that matter in this category>
Verdict:             <who should buy, who should not>
Disclosure:          <affiliate or vendor relationship>
Update date:         <when to re-check prices and models>
```
